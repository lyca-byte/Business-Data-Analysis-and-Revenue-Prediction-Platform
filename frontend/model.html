What is Linear Regression?

Linear Regression models the relationship between input features and a continuous target as a weighted sum:

Revenue = w₁×marketing + w₂×advertising + ... + w₇×prev_revenue + b

The algorithm learns the weights (w) and intercept (b) that minimise the sum of squared errors between predictions and actual revenue values across all training samples.

Metrics Explained

MAE — Mean Absolute Error

The average of absolute differences between predicted and actual revenue. Interpretation: "On average, the model's prediction is off by $14,127." MAE treats all errors equally regardless of size.

RMSE — Root Mean Squared Error

The square root of the average squared error, in the same unit as revenue (USD). RMSE penalises large errors more heavily than MAE. Interpretation: "The typical error, with larger errors weighted more."

MSE — Mean Squared Error

The average of squared prediction errors. The unit is USD² (squared dollars), which makes it hard to interpret directly. MSE is most useful as an intermediate step to compute RMSE.

R² — Coefficient of Determination

Measures what proportion of revenue variance the model explains. R² = 0.885 means the model accounts for 88.5% of revenue variation. A value of 1.0 would be perfect; 0.0 means the model is no better than always predicting the mean revenue.

R² Score — What It Means

Our model achieves R² = 0.885. A score above 0.80 is generally considered a strong fit for a business prediction model. The remaining ~11.5% of variance represents factors not captured by the 7 input features — seasonality, market conditions, and other external variables.

Feature Coefficients (StandardScaled)

Coefficients are on StandardScaled features — each feature was rescaled to mean=0, std=1 before training. This makes coefficients directly comparable across features regardless of their original units. A larger absolute coefficient means greater influence on the predicted revenue.

Interpretation note: Coefficients reflect linear associations on scaled features. They do not prove causation, and features that are correlated with each other share their explained variance. Use these as directional indicators, not as independent effect sizes.
